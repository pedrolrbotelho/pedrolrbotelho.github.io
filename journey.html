  • Hi, visitor!

    I'm from Brazil, and I've been working hard to get into a top PhD program in EECS, CS, or ECE. Here's a brief overview of my journey so far.

    1. 2018 – 2023 São José dos Campos, Brazil

      Instituto Tecnológico de Aeronáutica (ITA) B.Sc. in Electronic Engineering

      ITA is Brazil’s top-ranked engineering school (acceptance rate of 0.98%), run by the Brazilian Air Force’s Department of Aerospace Science and Technology (DCTA). My senior thesis, ASAGYM (with Gabriel H. Gobi, advised by Prof. Marcos Maximo), is a Python library for reinforcement learning in aerospace operational scenarios, built on ASA, the Air Force’s strategic simulator.

      Outside the classroom: in 2018 I taught mathematics and physics at CasdVest, a tuition-free college-prep course for students from low-income backgrounds; from 2019 to 2022 I led ROBIO, a student project that built an electronic hand prosthesis driven by AI classification of nerve signals; and in 2020 I interned as a backend and DevOps engineer at the fintech Conta Zap, migrating a WhatsApp banking bot from IBM Cloud to AWS. In 2023, as a research intern at the Air Force’s Institute for Advanced Studies (IEAv), I built the multi-agent RL framework behind my thesis — agents reached a 91% successful engagement rate, and the framework is being integrated into the Air Force’s pipeline.

    2. 2020 – present Goiânia, Brazil

      Center of Excellence in Artificial Intelligence (CEIA) & AKCIT Research Scientist

      CEIA and AKCIT are national joint research centers with more than 1,100 researchers and US$100 million in investment. Over the years I have worked on real-time visual odometry for autonomous vehicles, on a diffusion model for singing-voice conversion for Moises (Music.AI, Apple’s iPad App of the Year 2024), on offline-to-online RL for safe pre-deployment training of humanoid robots, and, currently, on Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks for continual learning. Recent outputs of this collaboration include CAL-MOS (Interspeech 2026) and a submission to ICASSP 2027 on whether locality prevents forgetting in MOS prediction.

    3. 2024 – 2025 Lagoa Santa, Brazil

      Brazilian Air Force 1st Lieutenant, Electronic Engineer

      After graduating, I was commissioned as a 1st Lieutenant, Electronic Engineer, at the Aeronautical Materials Park of Lagoa Santa (PAMA-LS), the unit responsible for modernizing and maintaining the Air Force’s aircraft. I worked on the navigation system update for the EMB-314 (A-29 Super Tucano) and modernized the Neiva T-25 and military Cessna 208 fleets. In 2024, I finished first in the General Mechanics Course of the T-27M aircraft (Embraer EMB-312). In 2025, I left active duty to dedicate myself fully to research.

    4. 2024 – present Evanston, IL

      Northwestern University Visiting Student, Communications and Networking Laboratory

      In Prof. Igor Kadota’s group I designed fully decentralized multi-agent reinforcement learning for dynamic spectrum access: a Distributional Recurrent Dueling DQN that reaches up to 86% throughput with an average Jain’s fairness index of 0.91 across 52 wireless network settings, robust to jamming and ad-hoc topologies, and a neuromorphic follow-up that raises fairness to 0.96 at 97% throughput. The first paper won the Best Student Paper Award at WiOpt 2025 in Linköping, Sweden; the second, Neuro-Cognitive Radios, appeared at the NeurIPS 2025 workshop on AI for next-generation wireless (AI4NextG). In the same period I took part in the LatinX in AI mentoring program at NeurIPS 2024, mentored by Felipe Leno da Silva (Microsoft) and Shagun Sodhani (Google DeepMind).

    5. June 2026 New York, NY

      Machine Learning Summer School (MLSS NYC 2026) Columbia University

      Selected — about 200 participants out of roughly 1,200 applicants worldwide — for the Machine Learning Summer School at Columbia University, June 15–26, 2026. Our team won first place in the hackathon with MathDistill, an AI-for-Science pipeline that extracts theorems, lemmas, and hypotheses from mathematical papers, organizes them into a visual knowledge graph, and formally verifies each statement by generating Lean proofs.

    6. 2026 – present Pittsburgh, PA

      Carnegie Mellon University Research Assistant, Electrical and Computer Engineering

      In May 2026 I joined Prof. Guannan Qu’s group at CMU, working with Alex DeWeese on the foundational principles and mathematical guarantees of Locally Interdependent Multi-Agent Markov Decision Processes (LIMAMDPs) — currently, LIMAMDPs under stochastic communication modeled by log-distance path loss with log-normal shadowing. It is the question at the center of my research: how agents that only see and talk to their neighbors can coordinate reliably, with guarantees.
